What is an operating model misfit?

In short

An operating model misfit is the gap between how an organisation is structured to work and the conditions it now operates in. The organisation keeps running, but effort rises while output does not.

An operating model misfit is the gap between how an organisation is structured to work and the conditions it now operates in. The structure was designed for conditions that no longer exist. The organisation keeps running, but every process carries friction that was not there when the model was built. Effort rises. Output does not.

Why misfit stays hidden

Misfit rarely announces itself. Results decline slowly. Capable people work harder to deliver the same outcomes. Decision cycles lengthen. Exceptions multiply until the exception is the process. Leaders read these as performance problems and respond with performance tools: new targets, new incentives, restructures at the edges. The strain continues because the cause is architectural, not motivational.

Six symptoms

  1. Decision latency. The organisation works hard and achieves less. Initiatives complete on time and on budget, yet results do not materialise at the scale the effort warrants.
  2. Workaround drift. Leadership quality is not the constraint. Replacing individuals does not change outcomes, because the architecture — not the people — is the binding limit.
  3. Savings evaporation. Plans look coherent on paper, but the structure cannot carry them into practice. The gap between what is decided and what gets done widens with each cycle.
  4. Role mismatch. More meetings, more committees, more sign-offs — and less speed. The overhead of operating the model overtakes the value it produces.
  5. Local speed, system slowness. AI tools surface the misfit rather than create it. When automation is applied to a broken model, it accelerates the dysfunction and makes the structural limits measurable.
  6. Strategy drift. Results that once came easily now require sustained pressure to achieve. The model was built for conditions that no longer hold, and the strain is the signal.

What happens when an operating model is no longer fit for purpose?

An operating model that is no longer fit for purpose keeps producing output, so the failure is easy to miss. Cost rises before performance falls. Capable people compensate for the structure until compensation becomes the job. The organisation does not break. It slows, and the slowing is read as a performance problem rather than a design problem.

What are the signs of a misaligned operating model?

A misaligned operating model shows up as friction that no single function owns. Approvals cross more boundaries than the decision requires. Accountability sits in one place and authority in another. Exceptions outnumber the standard route. Each symptom looks local. Together they describe a structure built for conditions that have since changed.

Why AI is exposing it

AI did not create operating model misfit. It made misfit visible and measurable. When a technology raises the baseline of what is possible, structures built for the old baseline show their limits at once. Organisations that apply AI to a misfitted model automate their friction. The gap between what the structure allows and what the conditions demand becomes the defining constraint.

Resilience will not close the gap

The standard response to strain is resilience: absorb the shock and return to normal. That response assumes the prior state is worth restoring and that conditions will revert. Where conditions have changed permanently, resilience directs effort backward. Organisations exhaust themselves restoring conditions that no longer exist.

Stuart J. Green's position is that regeneration is the alternative. It treats the disturbance as information, retires what is structurally obsolete, and designs the model for the conditions that now hold. In natural systems this is a measured process, not a metaphor. Some systems require fire to release dormant capacity.
