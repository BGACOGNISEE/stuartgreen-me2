What the book argues

The Regenerate Leap addresses one condition: the previous operating model has structurally failed and there is no prior state worth restoring. Most leadership books assume crisis has an end and that recovery means returning to normal. This book begins where that assumption fails. The baseline has shifted. Bouncing back is the wrong goal.

The book sets out the REGENERATE framework, a methodology drawn from three decades of marine systems science. Natural systems do not recover from catastrophic disturbance by restoring what was lost. They redesign around the conditions that now hold. Some systems require fire to release dormant capacity. The book translates those patterns into enforceable strategy for boards and chief executives.

The framework

Three phases, ten steps.

  • RAZE, The Clearing: Recognise, Embrace, Ground. Retire what is structurally obsolete and name what failed.
  • ENRICH, Building Capacity: Empower, Navigate, Equip, Reframe. Build capacity during instability, not after it.
  • GROW, Emergence: Adapt, Transform, Evolve. Design what comes next for the conditions that exist now.

Common questions

Is The Regenerate Leap a resilience book?

No. The book's position is that resilience aims to restore a prior state, and that this is the wrong goal when the baseline has shifted. The book sets out regeneration as the alternative: redesigning the system for the conditions that now hold.

Do I need a science background to read it?

No. The science is the evidence base. The book translates patterns from natural systems into decisions boards and executives already face.

How does the book relate to the diagnostic?

The diagnostic scores the misfit between an operating model and current conditions in thirteen questions. The book supplies the methodology for acting on that score.
