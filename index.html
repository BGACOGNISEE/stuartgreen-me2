Science and strategy

What living systems teach us about institutional change

Living systems demonstrate that disturbance can produce recovery, adaptation or fundamental transformation.

Three decades working across marine ecosystems and the institutions responsible for managing them have shaped my approach to organisational change.

The Regenerate method draws on ecological thinking, institutional experience and organisational research to examine whether existing strategies, structures and capabilities remain appropriate for changing conditions.

The objective is not to imitate nature. It is to help leaders make better decisions about what to preserve, what to change and what to build next.

Author and methodology

The Regenerate Leap

Leading when returning to the old normal is no longer an option.

The Regenerate Leap explores how leaders can respond when the assumptions supporting their previous approach no longer hold.

Drawing on marine systems, institutional experience and personal encounters with irreversible change, the book develops a framework for recognising what must end, building new capability and designing what comes next.

The Regenerate Leap draws on professional experience, ecological thinking and the author's experience of irreversible personal change. These experiences informed the development of the method; they do not constitute independent scientific validation.

The approach is organised around three connected disciplines:

  • RAZE — Identify what no longer fits.
  • ENRICH — Build the conditions for change.
  • GROW — Establish what comes next.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between resilience and regeneration?

Resilience concerns a system’s capacity to respond to disturbance, which may involve persistence, adaptation or transformation.

In The Regenerate Leap, regeneration is used as a practical leadership framework for situations where rebuilding the previous operating model is neither possible nor desirable.

The distinction helps leaders consider when adaptation within existing arrangements is insufficient and more fundamental redesign may be needed.

How do Blue-Green Advisors, The Regenerate Leap and The Regenerate Framework relate?

Blue-Green Advisors is Stuart's specialist ocean advisory company. The Regenerate Leap is his book and leadership methodology. The Regenerate Framework provides a structured approach to examining institutional operating-model fitness. Stuart's wider board and operating-model advisory work is discussed and scoped separately.
