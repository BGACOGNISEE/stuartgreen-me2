Two practices, one problem

Ocean institutions and corporate boards rarely think they have anything in common, and in practice they fail the same way. A ministry runs a fisheries management system designed for a stock structure that has since moved. A bank carries a portfolio built around a theory of change its own evaluations stopped supporting years ago. A chief executive holds an operating model that was correct in 2019 and has been wrong ever since, without anybody quite naming the year it turned. In each case the instinct is to work the existing model harder, and in each case that turns out to be the expensive answer.

The science signature

Regeneration is a measured process, not a metaphor

Regeneration is not a metaphor borrowed from nature. It is a measured process that natural systems run after catastrophic disturbance. A serotinous pine cone opens only under the heat of a fire, releasing seed into ground the fire has cleared. The fire is the condition the forest evolved to require. A kelp forest flattened by a storm regrows into a structure suited to the conditions that now hold, not the ones that came before.

Stuart J. Green spent thirty years studying these systems as a marine scientist before applying their logic to organisations. An institution under compound disruption is not waiting to return to a prior state. It is being asked to release dormant capacity, and to regrow into a structure that fits what it now faces. That translation, from marine systems science to institutional strategy, is the work.

What connects marine science to operating model advisory?

Regeneration is a documented process in reef and fishery systems, and it differs from recovery. Recovery returns a system to a prior state, while regeneration builds a different system suited to conditions that have changed and will not change back. Thirty years of watching which coastal systems did which, and of advising the institutions that governed them, produced a method that transfers. The Regenerate Leap sets it out for institutional leaders.

Where does operating model misfit show up?

An operating model misfit is the gap between how an organisation is structured to work and the conditions it now faces. The structure was built for conditions that no longer hold, so effort produces less than it should and the organisation strains to maintain results. Read the full definition.

Why is AI exposing operating models?

AI did not break the operating model. It revealed a model that had already stopped fitting. AI raises the baseline of what is possible, which makes the existing structure's limits visible and measurable rather than hidden.

What is the Regenerate framework?

The Regenerate framework is a methodology for organisations whose previous model is structurally obsolete and where recovery to a prior state is not an option. It runs in three phases, Raze, Enrich and Grow, drawn from how natural systems regenerate after catastrophic disturbance.

What is the difference between resilience and regeneration?

Resilience aims to restore a prior state and optimises for stability. Regeneration assumes the prior state is gone and redesigns the system for the conditions that now hold. Resilience looks backward, regeneration looks forward.

What is the difference between The Regenerate Leap and Blue-Green Advisors?

The Regenerate Leap is the book and the method. Blue-Green Advisors is the firm that applies it, alongside three decades of ocean strategy work, for institutional clients. Leaders who want the method read the book or take the assessment. Organisations that need it applied to their own operating model engage the firm.
