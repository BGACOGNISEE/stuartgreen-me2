Ocean Advisory

Ocean Strategy, Governance and Institutional Delivery

Ocean programmes increasingly bring together environmental objectives, institutional reform, investment and technology.

Their success depends not only on the quality of the strategy but on whether the responsible institutions can implement it.

I work with development banks, governments, foundations, funds and ocean-linked organisations on that challenge.

Ocean advisory through Blue-Green Advisors ↗

When This Work Is Relevant

Institutions typically seek specialist advice when a significant decision is approaching or when an established programme is struggling to deliver its intended results.

  1. 01An approved programme lacks clear implementation responsibilities.
  2. 02Investment capital is available, but governance arrangements or the project pipeline require development.
  3. 03Fisheries management arrangements need to adapt to changing ecological or economic conditions.
  4. 04Monitoring systems produce information that is not being translated into effective decisions.
  5. 05Multiple institutions share responsibility without sufficiently clear accountability.

Areas of Advisory Work

Ocean and coastal strategy.

National and regional strategy designed to survive political and funding cycles, with governance, capital, technology and operations treated as one system.

Fisheries governance and management.

Management system design, monitoring, control and surveillance, and stock assessment capability, from Philippine municipal fisheries in the 1990s to regional reviews of AI and machine-learning applications in fisheries management.

Blue finance and investment pipeline development.

Structuring, pipeline design and additionality assessment, including being explicit about which concepts are not yet fundable and why.

Marine monitoring, data and traceability.

The data infrastructure that increasingly determines market access and regulatory confidence.

Development programme and portfolio design.

Multi-country programmes, grant-making architecture, results frameworks, performance review and phased, evidence-grounded exits.

Institutional governance and implementation.

Governance reform and decision support for ocean institutions under sustained strain.

Who this is for

Development banks and multilateral programmes carrying ocean portfolios that need coherence across fisheries, marine protected areas, coastal adaptation and blue economy investment, and that need concepts to reach investment-readiness.

Sovereign and philanthropic funds deploying capital into ocean and coastal systems, where the live questions are additionality, pipeline quality, grant-making standards and how to exit a region responsibly.

National ministries and fisheries agencies designing management, monitoring and compliance systems that have to hold across changes of government.

Ocean-linked business, including seafood supply chains, aquaculture operators and ocean technology ventures, facing traceability obligations, standards regimes and market access conditions.

Earlier work

Portfolio design and exit. Designed and managed a multi-country fisheries governance portfolio across Southeast Asia, Melanesia and Micronesia for the David and Lucile Packard Foundation, and supported the Foundation’s phased, country-by-country exit after many years of regional investment.

Programme building. Helped design and pilot Fish Forever’s founding cohorts across the Philippines and Indonesia.

Governance and standards. Supported Reef Check operations across Indonesia and the Philippines and the establishment of Reef Check Malaysia, and supported a World Bank-funded certification standard for the marine ornamental fish trade.

National systems. As Provincial Coordinator for a USAID-funded integrated coastal management programme in the Philippines, worked on coastal law enforcement, marine protected areas and the creation of provincial environment management offices.
